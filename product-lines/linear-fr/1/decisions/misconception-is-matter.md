Without this, a creator writing a unit would write its misconceptions last, after its cases and on the situation of each, so one belief would be stated twice, from two cases, another would only negate a sentence of the matter, and the teacher, given a correction with no root, would answer the learner's words rather than the reasoning that led them there.

The matter of a unit of `linear-fr` has two faces, both fields of its **lesson**, in this order: what is true, then what seems true and is not, its **misconceptions**; both are **published** and both are sourced. A misconception is a belief a learner who has read the matter could still hold: a short name, unique in its unit, the belief quoted, then, nested under it, its **root**, why it seems true, and its correction, which rests on the matter, and nothing else. A unit holds three blocks, its lesson, its cases and its instruction: the block that held the misconceptions after the cases goes. The creator writes the matter, then the misconceptions, then the cases, the order of creation; the order of rendering is the site's, which renders the matter, then the cases, then the misconceptions, each with its name, its belief, its root and its correction, so that no misconception is given away before the question. A review removes a misconception that only negates a sentence of the matter with no root a learner could follow, and rewrites as a general belief, or merges, one written on a case's situation or repeating another of its unit; the description of a course shows a good one and a bad one. A root that states a claim is traced in the concept's sources, with the corrections. The length of one teacher message counts the matter and its case's anchor and question, never the misconceptions. This changes the form of the unit that decision `unit-is-the-content-unit` set, and the fields that decision `sources-trace-the-concept` traces.

## Options ruled out

- A block of misconceptions of its own, before the cases: the form would not say that they are matter.
- The block after the cases, as before: it inverts creation; the reason given for that place, the misconceptions before the question they miss, concerns rendering, not writing.
- A misconception in one sentence, root first: the root could not be told from the belief, nor checked present.
- The former form, a belief and its correction, the root implicit: a belief that only negates the matter passed.
- A similarity test between a correction and the matter, to catch a mere negation: it would be noisy.
- The mandatory root alone, with no review: a hollow root (« on pourrait le croire ») passes.
- The site rendering a misconception without its name, or without its root: the name is what a case cites, and the root is what the learner's reasoning meets.

## Consequences

- A case writes no misconception of its own: those it makes tempting are in the lesson of its unit, and a misconception's name is what lets a case cite it.
- The rule that nothing is nested under a misconception is lifted for its two parts only, the root and the correction.
- The fields of a unit's lesson, the misconception's name unique in its unit, and its two parts are checked by test; whether a misconception only negates the matter is a review.
