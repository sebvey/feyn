Without this, an agent writing a concept would write the matter of each teaching message three times, in the file the site built, again in the explanation of each case of a file only the teacher read and a third time in the prose instructions that cut the concept into messages, so the three copies drift (an explanation repeating half or more of the text the site built, an item of « Pièges » restating a common mistake), and the site, which builds that file whole, publishes whatever the writer left there that was meant for the teacher.

The content of a course of `linear-course` is written once, by **unit**: one teacher message, one leaf of a concept's tree. A concept is one file, its **concept file** `concept.md`: its title, then a head, its **sections** and their units, a foot. Every heading of the file opens with the kind of its block and one kind always sits at one heading level, so that a human who opens the file reads its structure without knowing the spec: the title is the one the course map gives the concept, which keeps it too and against which the check compares it; the head is one heading above the written summary of the concept, and the foot a heading of its own above its blocks; a section holds one unit at least and is never itself a unit, even when it holds a single one. A unit is three blocks, in the order its creator writes them, teach, ask, instruct: its **lesson** (its matter, what is true, then its misconceptions, what seems true and is not, each named, with its root and its correction: decision `misconception-is-matter`), its **cases** (the unit's case, always asked, then its supplementary cases, among which the teacher picks, every case under its own heading, its title, with its anchor, its question, its expected answer and the names of the misconceptions of its unit it makes tempting: decision `cases-name-their-misconceptions`), and its instruction. Each block is **published** or **unpublished**, a property the spec fixes for the block in its form, never one a course writes; a block's heading is its word alone. The site build is closed by default: it renders the published blocks and parts only, and nothing it does not know; the check refuses a line of a unit outside a known block or field, so an omission publishes nothing more, it fails. What a build can compose is not written: the objective comes from the course map. The form of the concept file (its parts, the blocks of a unit and their fields, their order, their presence, published or not, their readers, the cases of a unit, the length of a unit) is data of the spec, `course/form.yaml`, which names the words by their key in the lexicon of the course files and holds none of them; the checks and the site build read it. An annex is a concept file of the same form; a question often asked outside the course path is the matter of a unit of an annex (decision `aside-is-an-annex-unit`). This replaces the split of a concept into a file the site built and a file only the teacher read (decision `reader-facing-and-teacher-only-files`), whose filter was a file boundary: whatever sat in the first was built.

## Options ruled out

- Two files joined by the unit id, one published and one for the teacher: each unit written in two places, kept aligned by hand.
- One file per unit: four times the files, and a concept no longer read in one go.
- A third level of ids: two levels suffice once the leaf is a message.
- The form in prose in the spec's description: several programs read the form, and prose would write it once per reader.
- The words in `course/form.yaml`: a spec of another language would copy the structure to change the words.
- Telling the kinds of block apart by the style of their labels alone (bold published, italic unpublished): on a worked example, the structure still could not be seen.
- A unit of labelled fields under one heading: what each field publishes was to be known by heart, and a supplementary case was told from a part of its unit only by its parenthesis.
- One heading per field: seven headings for one line of text each.
- Blocks by teaching moment, a message and a correction: each mixes the published and the unpublished.
- The anchor kept in the lesson and the question in a block of its own, unpublished: a site reader would see a scene with no question.
- The supplementary cases under headings of their own, a block of misconceptions per case, or a title on the supplementary cases only: the cases of a unit would be read in two shapes, their misconceptions in several places, and the unit's case could not be cited.
- The concept's title in its file only: kept for later challenge, the course map still lists the concepts with their titles. The title in the course map only: the file would not say which concept it is.

## Consequences

- A creator writes a unit in one place and in one step, block after block in the order of writing; a unit that misses a mandatory block or field, holds its blocks out of order, or holds a line outside a block or a field, fails the check at once: a concept half written fails, which is wanted.
- The length of a unit is a reported signal, not an error: the check counts its matter and its own case's anchor and question in lines of 100 characters and reports a unit above twelve.
- `sources.md` traces the published fields that state claims, not the cases of a unit (decision `sources-trace-the-concept`); an unpublished block states nothing the published ones do not.
- A question the course asks again and again is written in the instruction of its unit or of its concept, or in a free section of the course plan when it holds for the whole course, never under a misconception.
- The frozen protocol still cites the words of the old form (its instructions, its bank, its drawing instruction, the fields of a case), kept apart from the lexicon of the course files until the protocol is brought back up to date (decision `teacher-frozen-until-the-protocol-brainstorm`).
