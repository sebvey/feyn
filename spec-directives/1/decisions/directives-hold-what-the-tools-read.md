Without this, an agent writing the spec of a product on the spec directives would find there the form of another product and the levels and the severity of one spec, written as prose no tool reads, and would write its rules to fit both logics at once, so that the directives bind every spec to choices only one of them made.

The spec directives hold what their tools read, each as a schema a test holds the files of a spec to: the manifest of a spec MAJOR, a rule model common to the course and to the products, the versions, and the tests of a spec's own files. The sieve for each sentence of the directives: does a tool of the directives read what it describes? If not, the sentence goes where its subject lives, the spec, or goes. The rule model holds what every rule file shares: a slug, a text in the third person, a why, a method, then a check or a criterion; whatever else classes a rule is a classification each spec declares in its manifest, by the owner of the rule file. A product line always has a course and declares its products freely, by name: the directives know the names of the products, not their files. The levels, the conformance clause of the course and the severity of a check, which decide which checks of a course block, move into the spec that uses them, with the code that derives the severity; the form of one product (its composition, its build, the form of its test scenarios) leaves the directives with its tests. The directives were written as a meta-spec for several specs while there is one; reworked in full now, before the spec of a product is written on them, they carry one logic.

## Options ruled out

- Cutting only what the spec of the teacher needs, the rest kept as an idea: half reduced, the directives would carry two logics while the spec of the teacher is written on them.
- Extending the current model, a field per need of each product: the model would grow the classes of every spec, and a second spec would inherit fields it has no use for.

## Consequences

- A spec states its classifications, its levels, its conformance clause and what follows from them in its own README; the directives cite them as examples.
- The bump of a spec change is written in the directives without the words of a level: a spec may write it in its own words, in its README.
