Without this, an agent would translate a French quote of the course into English, write a skill or a decision in French because the course is French, or write a course file in English because the repository is.

English everywhere, except what speaks to the learner, to the teacher or to the author of a French course. Language is a property of a spec, declared once (`language: fr` in the `spec.yaml` of its MAJOR), and so of the courses that follow it, not of the repository. A course of another language follows another spec, not a translation of this one (decision `lexicon-not-language-pack`). A spec speaks the language of its courses to its two human readers, the teacher and the course author: every file of the spec MAJOR they read is in that language, and so is every file the teacher reads wherever it lives, its implementation's included (`teacher-builder/<spec>/<MAJOR>/`, decision `teacher-implementation-folder`); what speaks to the tooling and the maintainer only stays English.

| English | French (for a French spec) |
|---|---|
| code, skills, agent workflows, CI workflows, scripts | `concept.md`, `sources.md` |
| root `CLAUDE.md`, teacher template | course glossary, storyline, the course's `plan.md` and `CHANGELOG.md` |
| the spec directives, repository docs, decisions (the spec's `decisions/` included), issues, labels | diagram text, the site's labels |
| the spec's `CHANGELOG.md`; file and folder names, `course.yaml` keys, the keys of the lexicons, the ids and slugs of the rule files, the keys of the teacher implementation's `composition.yaml` | what the teacher says, the words of the lexicons (`course/lexicon.yaml`, `products/teacher/lexicon.yaml`, `products/site/lexicon.yaml`) |
| the checks' code, the schemas' structure | the spec's texts for the teacher and the author: `products/teacher/README.md` (what is expected of a teacher, how a protocol rule is written), `course/README.md`, the `text` and `why` of `course/rules.yaml` and `products/teacher/rules.yaml` (the protocol rules of the teacher) and the `purpose` of their groups, the descriptions of `course/schemas/`, the messages of `course/checks/`, the MAJOR's `README.md`; what the teacher reads of its implementation: its instructions, the `what` and `when` texts of its `composition.yaml`, the learner templates |

An issue, a PR or a decision in English that quotes the course quotes it in French and never translates it. French text uses no em-dashes and no arrows. What the teacher says to the learner comes from the course, its protocol rules (its form marks and titles, word for word) and its implementation.

A maintainer phrasing quoted in a skill description's "Use for" may be in French, as a quote from course material is; everything else in a skill stays in English.

The protocol of `linear-fr` is written in French by this rule, not by exception: the spec is French, so are its protocol, its purposes and every text its teacher or its course author reads. It was first kept French as a bounded exception, because the old teacher was instructed and corrected in French and its v1 is a base inherited from `parcours-po`, changed by user story; the rule above absorbs that exception. A teacher or a course of another language follows another spec, with its own protocol and lexicons. The form marks and titles the learner sees are literal in the teacher's protocol rules, `products/teacher/rules.yaml`; its other phrases are the teacher implementation's (decision `lexicon-not-language-pack`). The spec's texts written in English before 2026-11-03 are translated as the stories that rewrite them pass (epic #450 for the two READMEs and the rule files, story #505 for the checks' messages and the schemas' descriptions, epic #222 for `composition.yaml`), never twice.
